A beginner's path to confident crochet

Learn Crochet

Six levels that take you from your first loop to making things that fit, hang well and last. Each step names one thing to follow, so you never have to choose.

6 levelsfirst loop to intermediate
5 to 8 monthsat 3 to 5 hours a week
Free firstone course, one site, two books

    Tap a level to jump to it. Mark a level finished when you are ready for the next.

    Before level 1

    You need very little. Buy these and nothing else for now.

    • YarnOne skein of smooth, light-colored worsted-weight yarn (the label shows a 4). Cotton or acrylic both work. Light colors let you see every stitch.
    • Hook5.0 mm (US H-8) or 5.5 mm (US I-9). Aluminum is fine. If your hand tires, try one with a thicker, cushioned handle.
    • Small thingsScissors, a blunt yarn needle with a big eye, and a few locking stitch markers.

    Left-handed? Every video below has a left-handed version or says so. If one doesn't, sit facing a mirror, or watch with the video flipped.

    Already making kits like The Woobles? You are somewhere in levels 4 to 5. Start at level 1 anyway and move quickly. The early levels fill in what the kits skip over: why the stitches look the way they do, and how to read a pattern.

    1

    First stitches

    About 2 to 3 weeks

    Hold the hook and yarn comfortably, and make rows of single crochet that stay the same width.

    Follow

    Sigoni Macaroni's free Learn to Crochet Masterclass

    Lessons 1 to 4: materials, hook, yarn and tension, slip knot and chain, single crochet.

    Teaches how to hold everything, how loops are made, and how to count a row. Free on YouTube with ads. Right- and left-handed.

    Make

    One square swatch: chain 21, then single crochet 20 stitches per row, for 20 rows. Count every row.

    It's plain on purpose. Straight edges mean you're not adding or skipping stitches.

    You're ready when

    • Every row has 20 stitches.
    • Both edges are straight against a ruler.
    • You can start a new swatch without the video.
    Saved on this device only.
    2

    Taller stitches

    About 3 to 4 weeks

    Learn the four stitches almost every pattern uses, and the turning chains that go with them.

    Follow

    The same Sigoni Macaroni course

    The lessons on double crochet, straight edges, half double crochet and treble crochet.

    Teaches how tall each stitch is, which chain you start each row with, and where the last stitch of a row hides.

    Make

    A cotton dishcloth in double crochet: chain 32, double crochet into the 4th chain from the hook, then 1 double crochet in each chain across (30 stitches). Each row: chain 3, turn, double crochet across, ending in the top of the turning chain. Stop when it is as tall as it is wide.

    Dishcloths are small, useful, and forgiving if the edges wander.

    You're ready when

    • Every row has 30 stitches.
    • You can tell single, half double, double and treble crochet apart by sight.
    • The edges have no gaps at the start of a row.
    Saved on this device only.
    3

    Reading patterns

    About 3 to 4 weeks

    Follow a written pattern from start to finish without watching a video, and fix small mistakes as you go.

    Follow

    Sigoni Macaroni's course, the lesson on reading and following patterns, then its Starlight Infinity Scarf

    Keep the Craft Yarn Council abbreviation list open while you read.

    Teaches what the stars and brackets mean, what the stitch count at the end of a line is for, and how to read the abbreviations.

    Own this book

    Everyday Crochet: The Complete Beginner's Guide by June Gilbank

    Photos for every stitch, tips for left-handers, symbol charts, ways to fix common mistakes, and 15 projects that get harder as you go. It stays useful through level 6. Try your library first.

    Make

    The Starlight Infinity Scarf from the course. Write the stitch count beside each row as you finish it.

    You're ready when

    • You finished a pattern with no video, and your counts matched.
    • You know that a US pattern's single crochet is a UK pattern's double crochet.
    • You can undo several rows and redo them without panic.
    Saved on this device only.
    4

    Working in the round

    About 4 to 6 weeks

    Crochet in circles, add stitches evenly, and make a granny square and a hat that fits.

    Follow

    Sigoni Macaroni's course, the lesson on joined versus continuous rounds

    Then the classic granny square tutorial by the same teacher.

    Teaches turning a ring into a flat circle, adding the same number of stitches every round, and building corners.

    Make

    Four granny squares, changing color every round. Then the free Simple Double Crochet Hat by Oombawka Design, a top-down hat worked in worsted yarn on a 5.5 mm hook.

    You're ready when

    • Your squares match in size and lie flat.
    • You can find the first stitch of any round.
    • The hat fits the head it was made for.
    Saved on this device only.
    5

    Your first toys

    About 4 to 8 weeks

    Make small stuffed toys (amigurumi) with firm fabric, round shapes and a neat finish.

    Follow

    PlanetJune's free tutorials, the Basics section first, then Amigurumi Essentials

    Teaches the magic ring, invisible decreases, closing a toy with no hole, and joining parts. Most videos come in right- and left-handed versions.

    Make

    A Woobles beginner kit

    The yarn, hook, pattern and videos come together, and the piece is pre-started, so you get a finished toy. Then make the same kit's pattern again from scratch with your own yarn.

    You're ready when

    • Your toy's stitches are tight enough that no stuffing shows when you squeeze it.
    • You can make a ball and close it so you can't find the end.
    • Matching parts, such as ears or arms, are the same size within 2 mm (1/16 inch).
    Saved on this device only.
    6

    Intermediate skills

    About 6 to 10 weeks

    Match a pattern's size, shape finished pieces, join them neatly, and use textured stitches.

    Follow

    Everyday Crochet, the chapters on stitch patterns and finishing

    Gauge: Sarah Maker's guide to measuring gauge. Blocking: Handy Little Me's beginner guide.

    Teaches why a swatch tells you how big a project will be, how to wash or steam a piece into shape, and how to join squares and edges.

    Make

    Marly Bird's Stitch Sampler Baby Blanket: nine squares, each a different stitch, plus a border. The squares are free on her blog; the compiled pattern is a paid download.

    You're ready when

    • You can swatch, measure and adjust your hook to match a pattern.
    • Your squares are the same size after blocking, and you joined them without gaps.
    • You can choose a pattern slightly above your level and finish it.
    Saved on this device only.

    What comes next

    You're an intermediate crocheter. Pick whichever direction pulls you. Each has one thing to start with.

    Toys

    The book to own: The Essential Guide to Amigurumi by June Gilbank, in right- and left-handed editions.

    Blankets

    The classic heirloom project: Sophie's Universe, free as a 20-part crochet-along.

    Wearables

    A first cardigan: Moogly's free Cosmos Cardigan.

    Mastery

    An outside check of everything above: the Crochet Guild of America's Masters programs. Start with Fundamentals.

    When you're stuck

    • Ask a person. r/CrochetHelp answers questions. Post two daylight photos (front and back), the pattern line, your hook size and what you expected.
    • Find a pattern or a note from someone who made it. Ravelry is free to join.
    • Look something up. The Craft Yarn Council keeps the standard abbreviations, yarn weights and hook sizes.
    • If a teacher doesn't click. Try Bella Coco's beginner videos on YouTube for the same lessons.
    • If your hand hurts. Stop, loosen your grip and rest. Pain that keeps coming back is worth a doctor's advice.
